# HARE — pitch and slides

Six slides. Few words on screen. Your voice carries the content.

Put the rabbit face on every slide you can. You have real screenshots — use
them, not clip art.

**Timing.** The whole thing is about 3 minutes: 40 seconds of talk before the
demo, the demo itself, then 40 seconds to close. A 2-minute version is at the
bottom.

---

## Slide 1 — Title

**ON SCREEN**

> # HARE
> Hands-on Adaptive Real-world Education
>
> *(the rabbit face, large, filling most of the slide)*

**YOU SAY** *(while it is up, 5 s)*

> "This is HARE. It is a learning companion for preschoolers."

Do not read the acronym out loud. It is on the screen. Saying it wastes
four seconds.

---

## Slide 2 — The problem

**ON SCREEN**

> ## Preschoolers do not learn sitting still.
>
> Screens ask them to sit still anyway.

**YOU SAY** *(15 s)*

> "Three to five year olds cannot hold one seated task for long. A lesson keeps
> going anyway, even when the child needs help or a break.
>
> Screen tutors make it worse. They ask a child who already cannot sit still to
> sit still and tap a screen.
>
> Some children find this much harder than others. Children with ADHD are the
> clearest case."

---

## Slide 3 — The solution

**ON SCREEN**

> ## Real objects. Real movement.
> ## HARE asks. It never scores.

**YOU SAY** *(20 s)*

> "HARE runs short physical missions with real blocks on a mat. A camera checks
> the answer.
>
> When a child stalls, HARE does not push on. It changes the shape of the
> lesson. Blocks become hops.
>
> And it asks the child what they need. A hint, more time, or a movement
> mission. It never scores attention, because looking away does not tell you
> whether a child is learning.
>
> Let me show you."

**Then run the demo.** Slide 3 stays up behind it.

---

## Slide 4 — Why a hare

**ON SCREEN**

> ## Why a hare, not a dog?
>
> Everyone knows how a real dog behaves.

**YOU SAY** *(10 s)*

> "Everyone knows how a real dog behaves. So a robot dog gets compared to one,
> and loses. Nobody has expectations of a hare.
>
> It also matters that dog fear is common in young children, and rabbit fear is
> not."

**Only if asked**, add: "The PARO therapy robot is a baby seal for the same
reason." Say PARO as "PAH-roh".

---

## Slide 5 — How it works

**ON SCREEN**

> ## How it works
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | Camera | counts real blocks |
> | Voice | hears "help me" |
> | Robot | hops the mission |
> | Screen | ten faces, no angry, no sad |
>
> *small line at the bottom:* dimOS · ElevenLabs · Deepgram · OpenAI · Arduino UNO Q · Devin

**YOU SAY** *(15 s)*

> "A camera counts the blocks. Voice hears the child ask for help. The robot
> hops the mission. The face has ten expressions, and on purpose there is no
> angry face and no sad face — HARE never scolds a child.
>
> It runs on dimOS, on an Arduino UNO Q, with ElevenLabs and Deepgram for voice.
> We built it with Devin and Codex."

Point at each sponsor name as you say it. Judges tick boxes fast.

---

## Slide 6 — What we will not claim

**ON SCREEN**

> ## What HARE does not do
>
> It does not diagnose.
> It does not score attention.
> No video of a child leaves the room.

**YOU SAY** *(15 s)*

> "Three things we will not claim. HARE does not diagnose anything. It does not
> measure attention, because a camera cannot. And the vision runs on the board,
> so no video of a child leaves the room.
>
> Speech still uses cloud APIs today. We are honest about that, and moving it on
> board is next."

**This slide wins trust.** Most teams overclaim. Saying the limits out loud is
what makes a sceptical judge believe the rest.

## Closing line

> "HARE adapts the mission, not the child. Thank you."

Stop talking. Do not add anything after it.

## The 2-minute version

Cut slides 4 and 6. Keep 1, 2, 3, 5, and the closing line.

Fold the honesty into the close:

> "HARE adapts the mission, not the child. It does not diagnose anything, and no
> video of a child leaves the room. Thank you."

## Slide design

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Six words per line, maximum | Judges read the slide or listen to you, never both |
| One idea per slide | Two ideas means they miss one |
| Warm off-white background | It matches the rabbit face. Dark slides fight your own product |
| The face on every slide | It is your strongest image. Use the real screenshots |
| No bullet lists of features | Nobody remembers a feature list |
| Font size 28 or larger | The back row exists |

## If the demo fails

Do not apologise twice. Say one line and use the three fallback lines from
`DEMO.md`:

> "The hardware is having a moment. Here is what it does."
>
> 1. It sees. HARE checks a real answer with a camera.
> 2. It moves. HARE turns blocks into hops when the child stalls.
> 3. It cares. HARE teaches soft hands, and never scolds.

Then show the backup video. Rehearse these three lines until you do not need
the slide.

## One thing to check before you present

If you name PARO on a slide, confirm the wording first. Search "PARO robot baby
harp seal design rationale". If you cannot confirm it quickly, drop the name —
the argument on slide 4 stands without it.
